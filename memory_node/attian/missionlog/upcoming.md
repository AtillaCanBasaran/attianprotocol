attianprotocol.net



My biggest suggestion is to make the Mission Log feel less like a collection of interesting things to do, and more like an actual open-world RPG built around one person's existence.
The important distinction:
- Main quests change the Subject's life or identity.
- Side quests create experiences, objects, or stories.
- Repeatable quests turn ordinary life into occasional gameplay.
- Meta quests change the archive itself, including its rules and characters.
- Multiplayer quests require other humans, preferably willing ones.
- World quests investigate, explore, or attempt to improve the world beyond the Subject.
- Corrupted quests exist because MUSE has apparently acquired administrative permissions.
I would keep Crafting, Seasonal, Scientific, Social, and Exploration as secondary tags. That way, a major crafting project can be a main quest, while a ridiculous five-minute crafting project can be a side quest.
There's also one structural issue worth fixing: Finish This Website is currently the first main quest. A website like Attian Protocol will never truly be finished. It needs a defined victory condition, such as Release Archive v1.0, followed by optional expansion quests. Otherwise you've invented an unbeatable final boss and called it personal organization.
Here is the expanded quest pool I'd actually consider implementing.

3. Repeatable quests // Everyday life, but with loot
Repeatable quests should never punish you for not completing them. They're opportunities to make ordinary weeks less interchangeable.
Quest	Repeatable trigger and objective	Reward
The Twenty-Minute Resurrection	Whenever an unfinished creative project is abandoned, work on it for twenty minutes and produce one visible change.	Procrastination Necromancy XP
Field Research: The Outside Exists	Take a different walking route, notice one unfamiliar detail, and archive it.	Minor Cartographic Revelation
Research Terminal: One New Brain Wrinkle	Explain one genuinely interesting scientific or cultural concept using the terminal's four-part format.	Knowledge Smuggling XP
Postcard From the Present	Capture one photo, sound, or paragraph representing an ordinary moment worth preserving.	One Future Memory Unlocked
The Social Save Point	Contact someone you haven't spoken with recently and exchange something more meaningful than a generic greeting.	Relationship Continuity Restored
Failure Is Also Data	When an experiment, craft, or plan fails, preserve the mistake and one thing learned from it.	Professional Disaster Archivist
The Tiny Expedition Generator	Pick one local destination you normally ignore and spend up to an hour investigating it.	Exploration XP; Routine Disruption
I'd make the rewards mostly ridiculous rather than numerical. Completing a repeatable quest could award things like One Less Ghost in the Unfinished Projects Folder, Minor Human Contact Restored, or Certified Witness of a Perfectly Ordinary Tuesday.
And, importantly, repeatable doesn't mean obligatory. No streak punishments. That's how an entertaining quest system mutates into an unpaid management position.

FEATURED META QUEST // META-001
The Archive Is Lying
An old memory has three narrators. None of them can be trusted equally.
Story: The Subject remembers an event. LILI has attempted an objective reconstruction. MUSE has produced something that might be classified as fan fiction. Unfortunately, all three accounts contain recognizable truths.
Requirements: Choose one genuine memory, write three interpretations without deliberately inventing factual evidence, and allow the visitor to switch between narrators.
XP: Epistemological Distress
Reward: Three Accounts, Zero Peace
LILI: The archive is not lying. It contains three incompatible interpretations of incomplete evidence. There is a difference. An extremely irritating difference.

This quest could also become an interactive element of the site rather than just text. It would bring the characters into the actual archival experience.

FEATURED MULTIPLAYER QUEST // PARTY-002
The Exchange of Quests
Two players. Two objectives. Absolutely no guarantee of dignity.
Story: The Subject temporarily delegates control of his mission log to another human. In return, he is granted the power to interfere with their day. The exchange is voluntary, which makes it slightly more ethical than most historical systems of governance.
Requirements: Both participants invent one harmless challenge for the other. Complete the challenges within an agreed time and exchange photographic, written, or verbal evidence.
XP: Social Contractual Nonsense
Reward: Quest Giver / Quest Victim
LILI: The Subject has invented cooperative game design and immediately used it to outsource bad decisions. At least consent was documented.

There's potential for multiplayer achievements to unlock for everyone involved, even if only your website tracks the event. It creates a fictional party history without requiring user accounts or a complicated multiplayer backend.

Because not every mystery needs a sensible origin, and MUSE deserves at least one persistent administrative nightmare.
The underlying design principle I'd use is that every completed quest should leave something behind: an object, photograph, friendship, story, new skill, memory, or a change in the world. Even the stupid ones.
And achievements should unlock based on what actually happened, not necessarily on what the quest was trying to accomplish. A failed attempt to photograph a pigeon might instead produce an unexpectedly memorable conversation. The mission fails; a secret achievement unlocks.
That is much closer to how life behaves, unfortunately.
